Where the port stands

The work so far was aimed at two questions: does the game fit, and does it run. Both have partial answers. The game boots to its main menu in PCSX2 at 32 MB, reads maps the PC converter has relocated, allocates its game state and plays with a scripted player. The campaign's smallest level plays its scripts and AI. Separately, a stand-alone sample proved that the Graphics Synthesizer can draw a Halo level with its lightmaps, detail maps, bump relief, reflections, fog and a skinned character at 60 fps, and documented how.

The hurdles

Ten things stand between the current build and a campaign play-through. Each row says what was measured, what the plan does about it and which milestone owns it. The first two decide whether the project is feasible at all; the rest are work.

The roadmap

Seven milestones from here. The order follows the dependencies: the simulation has to be fast enough before a renderer is worth writing against it, the converter's formats decide what the renderer draws, and the disc comes last because until then PCSX2's host: device stands in for it. Months are the plan's estimates for one experienced developer working full time, counted from now, not calendar dates. The three gates are decisions: each is passed on a real console.

M1

Gate 1: the simulation runs on a PS2 in budget

in progress
months 0 to 3 · source/, port/ps2/src, tools/ps2/pcsx2_bench.py · depends on nothing

The project's first real decision. c20 is three times over budget on emulated cycles, and the emulator reads low. The work is to measure on a console, then take the biped and machine updates apart. Everything here also makes the PC build slightly faster, so it lands on the Linux build first where saved films show whether behaviour moved.

Implementation. A float header maps fmod, sin, cos, floor, pow to their float forms and fmod(x, 1.0) to x - floorf(x); the build adds -fsingle-precision-constant and -Wdouble-promotion, and objdump | grep __adddf3 counts what is left. The biped update is profiled per phase (animation sampling, physics, collision) with the benchmark's section marks. Keyframe lookup keeps each channel's last index. matrix_math.c and real_math.h get VU0 macro-mode bodies behind the same C functions, as the sample's vu0_skin does. The collision BSP walk's working set moves to the 16 KB scratchpad.

Gate 1 · On a real PS2, a typical c20 tick takes 15 ms or less, with a written plan for the worst cases (large fights, many effects). If the ratio of hardware to PCSX2 makes this unreachable, the open question of a 128 MB or lower-frame-rate target is reopened before any renderer work.
M2

The converter fits every campaign level

pass 1 done
months 1 to 5 · tools/ps2/convert_map.py (or port/tools/ps2_map_convert.c), source/physics, source/hs, source/sound, source/bitmaps · depends on M1's gate for the go-ahead, and on M3's vertex format decision

Every pass is already prototyped and measured by converter_prototype.py; this milestone makes them write a map. The order follows the saving per unit of work: collision first (0.9 to 1.3 MB a level, a game-code change in collision_bsp*.c), then animations (1.0 to 1.9 MB, no game-code change because the engine already decodes its keyframe format), then the LOD, scripts and sound records. The vertex formats come last because they are the renderer's input: 16-byte model vertices (16-bit position, packed normal, 16-bit UV, node and weight) and 20-byte BSP vertices, which the converter can also pre-arrange as VIF unpack data.

Textures become 4-bit and 8-bit CLUT in GS layout with the top mip dropped, bump maps left out, cube maps as small sphere maps. Sounds become VAG with the music and long dialogue flagged for streaming. A budget report per map, like cache_file_report, closes the loop with map_budget.py.

Gate 2 · All ten campaign levels load in PCSX2 at 32 MB and play 300 ticks each with the scripted player, with no TLB miss and at least 1 MB of the 32 never taken. The projected peaks: a10 14.11, a30 14.08, a50 14.04, b30 13.74, b40 14.42, c10 13.88, c20 11.34, c40 14.32, d20 14.39, d40 14.12 MB.
M3

The renderer

not started
months 3 to 14 · source/rasterizer/ps2/ (new), port/ps2/vu1 (new), port/ps2/src/ps2_d3d_null.c retired · depends on M1's gate; its vertex formats feed M2

The largest piece and the one the sample de-risked. The backend implements rasterizer.h directly, never Direct3D: the EE builds REGLIST packet chains, VU1 microprograms transform, skin, light, clip and fog, the GS blends. The sample's findings carry over as rules: one A+D packet and one REGLIST per mesh (72 bytes a textured triangle, not gsKit's 680 cycles each); 16-bit 512x448 buffers with dithering; multiply passes through alpha-indexed palettes; the lightmap as a luminance page plus per-vertex hue; fog on the base pass with a scaled FOGCOL; the clear, the VU1 stream and the path-3 queue ordered by FINISH; a 1,400-pixel guard band.

The milestones run in the order a player meets them. Menus and text come first because nothing starts without them. The environment and models cover most of every level. The effects, water, glass and camouflage are what the campaign's set pieces need (a30's beach, a50's cloaked elites, b40's snow, c10's swamp and flashlight). Dynamic lights become per-vertex on VU1; lens flare occlusion becomes a ray cast against the collision BSP, which also deletes a 256 KB result table. Shadows render to a small GS target.

Textures are a cache, not resident: an EE-side cache of about 4.8 MB fed by the IOP, and a GS-side working set of about 2.3 MB uploaded through path 3 by the frame's needs, with predicted_resources.c as the prefetch list.

Milestone exit · The Pillar of Autumn is played from the first cinematic to the escape pod in PCSX2 with every surface drawn by the PS2 backend, at 30 frames a second, and the same build runs on the console without a visible fault. Not a gate: the work continues level by level in M6.
M4

Sound, the pad and the memory card

not started
months 8 to 11 · source/sound/sound_ps2.c (new), port/ps2/irx (new), port/ps2/src/ps2_input.c, saved games · depends on M2's VAG output; can overlap M3

A campaign cannot be played through without these three, and sound is more than atmosphere: scripts wait on dialogue and the cinematic audio. sound_ps2.c implements platform_sound.h as sound_dsound_xbox.c did, driving an IOP module (audsrv extended, or a custom IRX) that plays VAG voices from the 2 MB of sound RAM and streams music and long dialogue from the disc. The EE keeps the volume, pan and pitch maths of the 3D sounds. With 48 voices against the Xbox's 64, the sound manager gets a priority limit; the obstruction ray casts go round-robin. Reverb waits.

Input is libpad with the multitap left for later; the DualShock 2's white and black buttons map as L1 and R1. Saves compress the game state with the game's zlib onto the memory card. The capacities frozen in M2 are what make the save layout stable.

M5

The disc and streaming

not started
months 11 to 14 · tools/ps2 (ISO build, "ninja ps2_iso"), port/ps2/src (cdrom0: files), the converter's layout pass · depends on M2, M3's texture cache and M4's streaming channel

Until here PCSX2's host: stands in for the disc. The retail game has a DVD at 3 to 5 MB/s, slow seeks and no hard disk. A level start loads about 14 MB of tags in 3 to 5 seconds, which is fine; what is not fine is a seek storm during play. The converter places each BSP's textures, geometry and sounds together, the IOP reads ahead from predicted_resources.c, and a BSP switch is one contiguous read. The ISO is built from converted maps uncompressed, within a single-layer DVD, and boots on a console through its homebrew loader or a burned disc.

Gate 3 · The Pillar of Autumn boots from a disc image on a real PS2 with picture, sound and a pad, and plays to its end without a stall over one second outside the loading screen. From here the project is a campaign pass, not an engine port.

M7

Performance and finish

ongoing from M1
months 3 to 24 · source/ai, source/objects, port/ps2, docs · runs beside M3 to M6, driven by the profiles from each level

Gate 1 buys a typical tick; the campaign needs the worst ticks too. The levers, in order of how much they change behaviour: the AI timeslice given to fewer actors, perception staggered by distance with a phase per actor, movement and looking at 15 Hz for actors no player sees, lower actor limits in the converted scenarios where an encounter allows. Each lever is compared against the saved films. Particle and contrail updates move to a VU0 loop with per-level caps. Hot files compile at -O2 and the rest at -Os for the 16 KB instruction cache. The debug overlay shows free memory, pool peaks and tick time by subsystem, on hardware.

The finish is what a user needs: NTSC and PAL, 480p as an option, a README for players (convert your own disc, build the image) and for builders, and CI building the ELF with the ps2dev image and booting the menu in PCSX2.

What to do next week

  1. Get a console on the desk with FreeMcBoot and ps2link, and run pcsx2_bench.py's ELF on it for c20 and bloodgulch. That one number, the ratio of hardware to PCSX2, decides the shape of M1.
  2. Land the float fixes on the Linux build with -fsingle-precision-constant and the float libm header, and compare saved films. They are the cheapest tick savings on the list and they are needed on every platform.
  3. Profile the biped update on c20 by phase with the benchmark's section marks, so M1's work is aimed at the right third of the 27 ms.
  4. Choose the animation tolerance by looking at the cinematics on the PC, which unblocks the converter's largest pass with no game-code change.
